## Introduction to Bahasa Melayu
**Bahasa Melayu (Malay language)** is the national language of Malaysia and Brunei, and closely related to Bahasa Indonesia.
It is known for its logical grammar, easy pronunciation, and friendly tone.
No verb conjugation, no gender, and simple tenses make it one of the easiest Asian languages to learn for English speakers.

### Key Characteristics
- **Phonetic spelling:** what you see is what you say.
- **Simple structure:** Subject–Verb–Object order.
- **No plurals or gender:** context defines quantity.
- **Politeness markers:** used instead of verb changes.

## Basic Grammar Overview
Malay grammar is **analytic (no inflections)**. Meaning is built through **word order** and **particles**.

### Sentence Structure
**SVO pattern (Subject–Verb–Object)**  
> Saya makan nasi. — I eat rice.

Add time adverbs for tenses:  
- *Saya makan nasi semalam.* — I ate rice yesterday.  
- *Saya akan makan nasi.* — I will eat rice.

### Negation
- **tidak** — for verbs/adjectives  
- **bukan** — for nouns  

> Saya *tidak suka* kopi. — I don’t like coffee.  
> Ini *bukan* buku saya. — This is not my book.

### Possession
> buku saya — my book  
> kawan awak — your friend
